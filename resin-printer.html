Resin 3D Printer
(SLA) Operating Guide

FORMLABS FORM 4 PREFORM SOFTWARE LIQUID RESIN, GLOVES REQUIRED

Overview

What This Machine Does

This is an SLA (stereolithography) resin printer. Instead of melting filament or cutting with a laser, it uses a light source to selectively cure liquid photopolymer resin, layer by layer, into a solid part inside a resin tank. It produces much finer detail and smoother surfaces than FDM printing, at the cost of a genuinely different set of hazards: the material is a liquid chemical until it's cured, not a solid spool. Every step in this guide assumes you treat it that way.

Overview

Equipment You'll See Around It

The Printer (Formlabs Form 4)

The main unit with the orange-tinted cover. The orange tint blocks ambient UV/light from prematurely curing resin in the open tank, it isn't just styling. The touchscreen on the top-back corner controls the print queue and settings.

Resin Cartridges

Sealed 1L cartridges that dock into the labeled L and R bays on the side of the printer. The printer weighs each cartridge automatically to track remaining resin, you don't need to check levels manually.

Form Wash (IPA Wash Station)

A separate unit with its own hinged lid, used to bathe finished parts in isopropyl alcohol (IPA) and agitate them clean of uncured resin. The "flammable solvent" warning on its lid is about the IPA inside, not the printer itself.

⛔ Never Do This

  • Never pour liquid resin, or IPA that's washed resin off a part, down a drain. Both need proper disposal, not household plumbing.
  • Never handle liquid resin without nitrile or neoprene gloves. Latex does not reliably protect against it and can fail on contact with IPA.
  • Never look directly at the UV curing light, whether that's inside the printer during a print or in a curing chamber afterward.
  • Never leave the printer's cover open longer than necessary. Stray light can prematurely cure resin sitting in the open tank, ruining it.
  • Never handle IPA near heat, sparks, or open flame. Its vapor is flammable.

⚠ Safety First

  • Always wear gloves and safety glasses when handling resin, whether it's liquid in the tank, wet on a fresh print, or on the build platform.
  • Avoid skin contact with uncured resin. It's a recognized skin sensitizer, meaning repeated exposure can trigger a reaction even in someone who was previously fine with it.
  • Do not look directly at UV light during printing or curing.
  • Clean spills immediately using paper towels and isopropyl alcohol. Uncured resin left on a surface stays reactive and stays a contact risk until it's wiped up.
  • Dispose of resin waste properly. Never pour it down the drain, ask a lab assistant or Prof. Chika about the correct disposal process if you're unsure.
  • Work in a ventilated area, particularly around the wash station.

Before You Start

Nitrile or neoprene gloves and safety glasses are on hand (not latex)
Resin cartridge is loaded and shows a sufficient level on the touchscreen
Resin tank and build platform are free of cured debris or old resin
Your file is sliced and ready in PreForm
Form Wash has clean IPA at the correct level, if you'll be washing right after
Paper towels and IPA are within reach for spill cleanup
The area is ventilated and away from heat, sparks, or open flame

Step-by-Step Guide

01
Prepare Your File in PreForm
  • 1Import your 3D model (STL) into PreForm, Formlabs' free slicing software.
  • 2Select the correct printer profile and the resin type actually loaded in the machine.
  • 3Orient the part and generate supports, PreForm can auto-generate these, or you can adjust them manually for tricky geometry.
  • 4Slice the file and send it to the printer over USB, Wi-Fi, or Formlabs Dashboard.
02
Load Resin and Prepare the Printer
  • 5Check the resin level for your job on the touchscreen.
  • 6If loading a new cartridge, dock it into the labeled L or R bay until it clicks into place.
  • 7Check the resin tank and build platform for leftover cured debris, scrape clean with the plastic scraper if needed.
  • 8Close the orange cover fully before starting.
03
Start the Print
  • 9Confirm the job on the touchscreen if it doesn't begin automatically.
  • 10Leave the cover closed once printing starts. Opening it risks a failed print and exposes the open resin tank to stray light.
04
Let It Run

Resin printing needs less hands-on attention than FDM or laser work, the process is fully enclosed. That's not a reason to ignore it entirely.

  • 11Check on it periodically rather than leaving the building entirely, and don't open the cover mid-print.
05
Remove Your Part
  • 12Put on gloves before opening the cover once the print completes.
  • 13Remove the build platform and use the removal tool or scraper to separate the part, working over a drip tray since it's still coated in wet, uncured resin.
06
Wash
  • 14Form Wash:Place the part in the basket and run it for the recommended time for your specific resin, roughly 10-15 minutes is typical with fresh IPA, but check Formlabs' published wash settings for the exact resin in use.
  • 15Manual wash:Submerge and agitate the part in a container of IPA for a similar duration, gloves on, in a ventilated area away from heat or sparks.
  • 16Let the part air-dry fully afterward. Use compressed air to clear any trapped IPA or resin from internal cavities or channels if available.
07
Cure

Don't skip this. A washed part isn't a finished part, it looks solid but hasn't fully cross-linked yet, and won't reach its intended strength until it's cured.

  • 17Form Cure:Place fully dried parts evenly on the turntable and select the time and temperature preset for your exact resin, these vary significantly by material.
  • 18Manual curing:Without a Form Cure, use direct sunlight or a UV lamp/chamber, rotating the part partway through so every face gets even exposure.
08
Remove Supports and Finish
  • 19Once fully cured, snip away support structures with flush cutters.
  • 20Sand or clean up support marks as needed.
09
Shutdown and Cleanup
  • 21Wipe down the build platform and any resin drips with paper towels and IPA.
  • 22Check the resin tank for cured fragments, scrape clean if needed.
  • 23Close the printer's cover.
  • 24Log your print in the usage sheet if one exists.

Reference

Troubleshooting Quick Reference

Print fails or detaches from the build platform

Fix

Check the platform is fully seated, confirm resin level wasn't too low, and consider adjusting supports or adding a raft in PreForm.

Cartridge not recognized or not dispensing

Fix

Reseat the cartridge in its bay, check the valve for a clog, ask a lab assistant if it persists.

Part still feels sticky or tacky after washing

Fix

Run another wash cycle. Check tight recesses and internal channels didn't trap uncured resin.

Finished part is weak or brittle

Fix

It's likely under-cured. Confirm the curing step ran for the correct time and temperature for that specific resin.

Resin spill on the machine or table

Fix

Clean immediately with paper towels and isopropyl alcohol, gloves on. Dispose of used towels properly, don't rinse anything down a drain.
